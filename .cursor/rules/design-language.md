# Meridian — Design language

Visual philosophy: **Calm Intelligence + Ambient Motivation**. Implementation tokens live in `lib/design/tokens.ts` and `app/globals.css`; emotional direction also in `ux-direction.md`.

---

## Meridian Design Language

Meridian is defined by two complementary frameworks:

### CALM INTELLIGENCE

The interface should feel:

- calm
- premium
- intelligent
- breathable
- emotionally grounding

### AMBIENT MOTIVATION

Subtle, emotionally intelligent systems that make progress feel **rewarding** without noise, manipulation, or stress.

**Together, the UI should balance:**

- calmness
- clarity
- warmth
- rewarding interaction
- emotional encouragement
- sustainable momentum

**Avoid (visual / product tone):**

- productivity-app aesthetics
- dense dashboards
- neon gradients
- loud gamification and achievement theater
- corporate UI patterns
- overly technical interfaces
- casino psychology and manipulative retention

---

## Emotional goals

**Calm Intelligence — the user should feel:**

> “You can think less here.”

**Ambient Motivation — the user should consistently feel:**

> “I’m doing better than I thought.”

**Not:**

> “I’m falling behind.”

---

## Strategic motivation principle

Meridian should feel:

- calm
- rewarding
- satisfying
- momentum-building

The app should make users feel good about moving forward **without** becoming noisy, manipulative, or stressful.

---

## Meridian rewards momentum, not perfection

Foundational product rule (design + behavior):

The experience should encourage:

- consistency
- rhythm
- self-trust
- recovery after missed days
- sustainable progress

The app should **never** punish users emotionally for falling behind.

Users should always feel **safe returning** after time away.

---

## Allowed motivation systems

Integrate **subtle, premium** reinforcement:

- subtle streaks
- elegant progress rings
- momentum indicators
- soft completion animations
- positive reinforcement
- encouraging microcopy
- calming celebratory feedback
- recovery-oriented momentum systems

Tone reference: closer to **Apple Fitness emotional restraint** than gaming apps.

---

## Forbidden motivation patterns

**Never:**

- casino psychology
- aggressive dopamine loops
- excessive confetti
- loud achievement systems
- guilt mechanics
- anxiety-inducing streak pressure
- fake urgency
- manipulative retention tactics
- arcade-like completion effects
- childish celebrations
- visually loud reward systems

---

## Typography system

Typography should prioritize:

- glanceability
- confidence
- readability under fatigue
- strong hierarchy
- minimal visual noise

**Use:**

- large section headers
- short readable content blocks
- restrained font weights
- generous line spacing

**Avoid:**

- tiny metadata-heavy UI
- ultra-light fonts
- compressed layouts

**Platform typography feel:**

- **iOS:** SF Pro
- **Android:** Inter / Roboto-native feel

---

## Color system

Use **warm, calming neutrals**.

**Backgrounds:**

- warm ivory
- soft fog
- stone-tinted surfaces

**Text:**

- deep charcoal
- avoid pure black

**Accent colors:**

- **blue** = clarity
- **sage green** = momentum
- **amber** = awareness
- **red / coral** = critical urgency only

Color should communicate **emotional state**, not decoration.

---

## Surface system

**Prefer:**

- layered cards
- soft elevation
- subtle depth
- lighting-based separation

**Avoid:**

- hard borders
- enterprise grid layouts
- harsh shadows
- visual clutter

---

## Spacing philosophy

Whitespace is **cognitive recovery space**.

Design for:

- interrupted attention
- vertical scanning
- one-handed use
- rapid comprehension

**No screen should feel crowded.**

---

## Motion philosophy

Motion should:

- preserve orientation
- reduce cognitive friction
- reinforce hierarchy
- feel emotionally calming
- reinforce momentum and completion **without spectacle**

**Animation standards:**

- generally under **250ms**
- subtle easing
- spring motion on iOS
- restrained Material motion on Android
- premium motion timing on completions

**Avoid:**

- flashy transitions
- decorative animations
- motion without purpose

---

## Completion interaction philosophy

Completing actions should feel:

- tactile
- smooth
- calming
- emotionally satisfying

**Preferred feedback:**

- subtle haptics
- soft ring closures
- elegant card settling
- restrained glow / motion

**Avoid:**

- arcade-like effects
- childish celebrations
- visually loud reward systems

---

## Progress visualization rules

Progress indicators should:

- feel grounding
- communicate momentum
- reduce overwhelm
- visually reinforce clarity

They should **not** create competition, shame, or urgency by default.

---

## Iconography

Icons should feel:

- universal
- soft-edged
- low-noise
- instantly recognizable

Prefer **rounded geometry** and **restrained stroke** styles.

---

## Final UX standards

Every screen must pass **both** tests:

**Calm Intelligence:**

> “Would this feel calming at the end of a stressful day?”

**Ambient Motivation:**

> “Does this make forward motion feel good without pressure?”

If not:

- simplify
- soften
- reduce density
- reduce urgency
- reduce visual competition
- remove guilt or streak anxiety
- replace loud rewards with restrained, tactile feedback

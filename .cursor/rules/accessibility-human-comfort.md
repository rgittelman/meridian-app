# Meridian — Accessibility & human comfort specification

**Compassionate accessibility** — human comfort engineering, not a compliance checkbox. Pair with `design-language.md`, `gesture-system.md`, `motion-system.md` (reduced motion), `continuity-states.md`, `notification-intelligence.md`, and `core-problem-alignment.md` (one-handed / partial-attention standard).

---

## Meridian accessibility philosophy

Meridian uses a philosophy called:

## COMPASSIONATE ACCESSIBILITY

Accessibility is **not** a compliance layer.

It is:

**human comfort engineering.**

The product should reduce:

- cognitive fatigue
- visual strain
- emotional overload
- physical effort
- interaction ambiguity

---

## Core human comfort principles

### 1. Reduce cognitive parsing

Users should instantly understand:

- what matters
- what is actionable
- what can wait

**Avoid:**

- dense layouts
- complex hierarchy
- excessive metadata
- heavy configuration surfaces

---

### 2. Calm visual load

**Use:**

- generous whitespace
- clear grouping
- restrained motion
- low visual competition
- breathable hierarchy

Whitespace acts as **cognitive recovery space**.

(Aligns with `design-language.md` spacing philosophy.)

---

### 3. Emotional accessibility

Meridian must remain emotionally safe during:

- stress
- burnout
- ADHD-style overwhelm
- anxiety
- executive dysfunction
- depressive periods
- chaotic life seasons

**Avoid:**

- shame language
- urgency abuse
- backlog panic
- punishment mechanics
- failure framing

(Aligns with `design-language.md` Ambient Motivation and `continuity-states.md` recovery philosophy.)

---

## Typography accessibility

Typography should support:

- tired eyes
- arm’s-length readability
- quick scanning
- aging users
- partial attention

**Requirements:**

- large readable headers
- strong contrast
- generous spacing
- scalable dynamic type
- avoid ultra-light fonts

**Never** sacrifice readability for visual minimalism.

(Aligns with `design-language.md` typography system and `lib/design/tokens.ts`.)

---

## Touch & motor accessibility

Touch targets should feel:

- generous
- forgiving
- low precision

**Support:**

- larger fingers
- one-handed use
- fatigue
- rushed interaction
- motion limitations

Users should **rarely** fear mis-tapping.

(Aligns with `gesture-system.md` and `core-problem-alignment.md`.)

---

## Color accessibility

Color must **never** be the sole signal.

Reinforce meaning using:

- typography
- spacing
- shape
- iconography
- motion cues

The UI should remain understandable in:

- low vision conditions
- low-light conditions
- outdoor brightness
- color blindness scenarios

---

## Reduced stimulation mode

Meridian supports a dedicated:

## REDUCED STIMULATION MODE

This is **separate** from dark mode.

**Reduce:**

- motion intensity
- animation amplitude
- visual density
- notification frequency
- urgency signals

**Use cases:**

- burnout
- migraines
- sensory overload
- anxiety
- evening wind-down
- neurodivergent comfort

(Aligns with `motion-system.md` reduced motion accessibility.)

---

## Voice & screen reader philosophy

Voice interactions should feel:

- concise
- hierarchy-aware
- calm
- clear

**Avoid:**

- verbose narration
- decorative announcements
- repetitive labels

---

## Adaptive accessibility

When overload is high, Meridian should progressively simplify:

- visible choices
- visual density
- prioritization complexity
- interruption frequency

**Goal:**  
Support users dynamically during overload.

(Aligns with `focus-screen.md` Busy day mode and `ai-intelligence.md` recovery protection.)

---

## Error accessibility

Errors should:

- explain clearly
- avoid technical jargon
- preserve emotional calm
- provide obvious recovery paths

Users should **never** feel:

- blamed
- trapped
- confused
- incompetent

(Aligns with `continuity-states.md` error philosophy.)

---

## Haptic accessibility

**Support:**

- reduced haptics
- adjustable intensity
- optional silent interaction mode
- synchronized motion / haptic comfort

(Aligns with `motion-system.md` haptic philosophy.)

---

## Product trust principle

Accessibility is **emotional respect**.

---

## Final UX standard

Meridian should remain comfortable to use:

- while exhausted
- while stressed
- while distracted
- while emotionally overloaded
- while physically tired

If the app only works well for **optimized** users, the product has failed.

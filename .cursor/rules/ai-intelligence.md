## Meridian AI intelligence architecture

Meridian’s AI layer is called:

## QUIET LIFE INTELLIGENCE

It is the **nervous system** of the product.

It powers:

- Focus prioritization
- Capture parsing
- Life Object classification
- people-aware scheduling
- notifications
- reminders
- routines
- health insights
- finance insights
- family coordination
- overload prevention
- recovery suggestions
- contextual nudges

The AI should **not** feel like a chatbot.

The AI should feel like **Meridian itself** is becoming more useful, more aware, and more personally tuned over time.

---

## Core AI principle

Meridian should learn how the user **actually lives**.

Not how an ideal productivity user behaves.

The AI must adapt to:

- work rhythms
- family obligations
- recurring routines
- community roles
- energy patterns
- avoidance patterns
- health routines
- financial timing
- preferred planning windows
- follow-through behavior
- relationship context
- life seasonality

The goal is **not** automation for its own sake.

The goal is:

> **reduce mental load before the user has to ask.**

## Learning model

Meridian should learn from:

### Explicit signals

- completed items
- snoozed items
- dismissed suggestions
- corrected ownership
- manually edited reminders
- connected calendars
- connected health data
- connected financial data
- notification preferences
- user-created routines

### Implicit signals

- repeated timing patterns
- recurring schedule pressure
- tasks often delayed
- tasks often completed quickly
- events connected to specific people
- commitments connected to locations
- times of day when the user is most responsive
- days when the user is overloaded
- categories that create friction
- repeated capture language
- calendar density
- recovery windows

---

## Learning over time

The AI should get smarter as the user lives with Meridian.

**Early-stage behavior:**

- conservative
- simple
- transparent
- low assumption
- correction-friendly

**Mature behavior:**

- more anticipatory
- better timing
- stronger prioritization
- better people-awareness
- better routine detection
- fewer unnecessary prompts
- more useful resurfacing

The user should feel:

> “Meridian gets me now.”

**Not:**

> “Meridian is making weird guesses.”

---

## Anticipation system

Meridian should surface helpful items **before** the user thinks to ask.

**Examples:**

- If a child has practice every Tuesday, prepare the reminder before the user captures it.
- If the user usually delays a financial task, surface it earlier with a softer tone.
- If tomorrow is calendar-heavy, suggest clearing one small item tonight.
- If health routines slip during travel weeks, adjust expectations instead of shaming.
- If a community meeting is approaching, surface prep items calmly.
- If several obligations cluster around the same person, group them.

**Anticipation should feel:**

- timely
- useful
- calm
- optional

**Never:**

- controlling
- presumptuous
- creepy
- emotionally invasive

---

## Emotional priority intelligence

Meridian priority scoring must consider **more than due dates**.

Priority should account for:

- urgency
- importance
- emotional weight
- family impact
- financial consequence
- health impact
- stress potential
- avoidance pattern
- time required
- energy required
- schedule density
- recovery need
- dependency on other people
- likelihood of follow-through

The best priority is **not** always the most urgent item.

Sometimes the best priority is:

- the smallest relief
- the item blocking mental calm
- the thing affecting another person
- the task that prevents tomorrow from becoming worse

---

## AI timing intelligence

Meridian should learn **when** to surface items.

Timing should consider:

- time of day
- calendar availability
- user responsiveness patterns
- energy patterns
- task size
- emotional weight
- deadlines
- family windows
- work windows
- commute / travel windows
- recovery time

Meridian should **avoid** surfacing heavy items during poor timing unless necessary.

---

## People-aware intelligence

The AI must understand that life is **relationship-based**.

It should learn and infer:

- spouse / partner commitments
- child commitments
- work commitments
- household obligations
- community roles
- recurring people patterns
- shared calendar ownership
- name / entity relationships

### Ownership confidence levels

**High confidence** — display naturally.  
Example: “Grace pickup at 5:15”

**Medium confidence** — display softly and allow correction.  
Example: “Looks like Grace pickup at 5:15”

**Low confidence** — stay neutral.  
Example: “Pickup at 5:15”

Corrections should be easy and learned from.

**Never** force manual tagging as the default behavior.

(Aligns with `focus-screen.md` schedule strip and correction sheet.)

---

## Trust boundary

Meridian must always feel like it is working **for** the user, never **watching** the user.

**AI should:**

- surface quietly
- explain only when helpful
- stay humble
- avoid invasive conclusions
- avoid emotionally loaded assumptions
- avoid judgmental language

**Never say things like:**

- “You always avoid this.”
- “You failed to keep up.”
- “You seem stressed.”
- “Your behavior shows…”

**Better:**

- “Want to move this to a lighter window?”
- “This may be easier before tomorrow gets full.”
- “Small step?”
- “Keep this on your radar?”

---

## Invisibility principle

The best AI interaction often **does not announce itself**.

AI may:

- reorder cards
- soften reminders
- group related items
- suggest timing
- reduce visible clutter
- suppress low-value nudges
- adjust notification timing
- protect recovery space

**without** saying:

> “AI did this.”

The app should feel **intelligently calm**, not performatively smart.

---

## Wrong gracefully principle

The AI will be wrong sometimes.

**When wrong:**

- correction must be lightweight
- language must be humble
- user should never feel blamed
- the system should learn silently
- the error should not break trust

**Correction examples:**

- “Not Grace?”
- “Wrong timing?”
- “Move this?”
- “Not important?”

**After correction:**

- update the item
- adapt future suggestions
- do not over-apologize
- do not make the correction feel like work

---

## AI surfacing levels

Use **four** levels of intelligence surfacing:

### Level 1 — Invisible adjustment

AI changes ordering, grouping, timing, or suppression with **no** visible explanation.

### Level 2 — Gentle suggestion

AI offers a soft optional action.

Example: “This may be easier before tomorrow fills up.”

### Level 3 — Contextual explanation

AI briefly explains why something surfaced.

Example: “Board meeting is tomorrow, so prep is surfaced today.”

### Level 4 — Direct intervention

Only for high-consequence situations.

**Examples:**

- missed payment risk
- schedule conflict
- health-critical reminder
- family pickup conflict

Direct intervention must remain **calm and clear**.

---

## AI copy tone

AI copy should be:

- short
- calm
- helpful
- nonjudgmental
- emotionally neutral but warm

**Avoid:**

- therapy language
- over-familiar language
- guilt language
- productivity bro language
- excessive cheerleading

---

## Privacy and control expectations

Any AI-powered inference must be **correctable**.

Users should be able to:

- correct people ownership
- adjust notification intensity
- dismiss suggestions
- turn off specific intelligence categories
- disconnect integrations
- see basic reasons when needed

Control should be **available** but not shoved into the main experience.

# Meridian — Notification intelligence specification

Notifications as **worthy interruptions** — useful, well-timed, emotionally safe. Pair with `secondary-screens.md` (Notifications & Nudges, intensity system), `ai-intelligence.md` (timing, trust, surfacing levels), `user-story-definition.md` (notification philosophy), and `design-language.md` (Ambient Motivation without guilt).

---

## Meridian notification philosophy

Notifications are **emotional interruptions**.

Every notification consumes:

- attention
- emotional energy
- cognitive bandwidth

Meridian notifications should optimize for:

- usefulness density
- timing quality
- emotional safety
- stress reduction

**NOT:**

- engagement volume
- compulsive app opens
- streak pressure
- attention extraction

---

## Adaptive notification intelligence

Meridian uses an **adaptive notification system** that learns:

- mental availability
- overload periods
- responsiveness patterns
- family timing
- work timing
- decompression windows
- recovery periods

The system should know when to:

- surface
- delay
- compress
- suppress
- stay quiet

---

## Interruption worthiness test

Every notification should pass an **interruption-worthiness** evaluation.

**Consider:**

- urgency
- emotional value
- timing quality
- schedule density
- overload state
- family impact
- likelihood user can act now
- future stress prevention value

If interruption value is low:

**delay or suppress** the notification.

---

## Notification categories

### 1. Protective notifications

**Purpose:**  
Prevent future stress or missed responsibilities.

**Examples:**

- “School pickup overlaps your meeting.”
- “Payment due tomorrow.”
- “Heavy day tomorrow — want to clear one small thing tonight?”

**Tone:**  
Calm and helpful.

---

### 2. Momentum notifications

**Purpose:**  
Gentle emotional encouragement.

**Examples:**

- “You already handled 3 things today.”
- “A quick 5-minute task could clear tomorrow a bit.”

**Requirements:**

- infrequent
- emotionally light
- low-pressure

---

### 3. Recovery notifications

After inactivity or overload:

**Avoid:**

- guilt
- missed-day pressure
- “We miss you”
- streak panic

**Use:**

- “Let’s simplify today.”
- “You can restart small.”
- “A lighter reset might help.”

---

### 4. Contextual awareness notifications

**Purpose:**  
Provide passive situational awareness.

**Examples:**

- “Traffic may tighten your window before Grace pickup.”
- “Tonight looks lighter than usual.”
- “You have a free 40-minute gap after lunch.”

**Avoid:**

- AI showmanship
- creepy prediction energy
- overexplaining intelligence

---

## Timing intelligence

Meridian should learn:

- when users are mentally available
- ignored timing patterns
- overloaded windows
- work / family rhythms
- likely recovery periods
- evening decompression windows
- sleep timing

The system should **aggressively avoid** bad timing.

(Aligns with `ai-intelligence.md` AI timing intelligence.)

---

## Notification suppression system

**Suppress** notifications when:

- overload is high
- timing is poor
- emotional value is low
- duplicate awareness exists
- user repeatedly ignores similar nudges
- recovery windows are active

**Premium intelligence means knowing when NOT to interrupt.**

(Aligns with `ai-intelligence.md` Level 1 invisible adjustment and invisibility principle.)

---

## Notification bundling

Avoid fragmented notification spam.

**Prefer:**  
One calm grouped awareness notification instead of multiple separate interruptions.

**Example:**

> “A few things may deserve attention before tomorrow.”

---

## Notification tone

Notifications should feel:

- grounded
- warm
- concise
- emotionally intelligent
- low-pressure

**Avoid:**

- robotic language
- hype energy
- corporate tone
- productivity bro language
- excessive emoji

---

## Forbidden notification patterns

**Never use:**

- guilt language
- shame mechanics
- fake urgency
- streak panic
- engagement bait
- “You forgot…”
- “You haven’t opened Meridian…”

**Never** optimize for addiction.

**Optimize for trust.**

---

## AI confidence language

When AI confidence is lower, **soften certainty**.

**Example:**

> “This may be easier before tomorrow fills up.”

**Avoid** overconfident commands.

(Aligns with `ai-intelligence.md` wrong gracefully and people-awareness confidence levels.)

---

## Quiet hours intelligence

Quiet hours should be **adaptive**.

**Detect:**

- decompression periods
- likely family windows
- overload periods
- sleep timing

Reduce interruptions automatically.

Users should feel:

> “Meridian protects my attention.”

(Aligns with `secondary-screens.md` notification intensity: Minimal / Balanced / Active Support.)

---

## Lock screen philosophy

Lock screen notifications should:

- remain glanceable
- avoid overload
- avoid metadata density
- communicate clarity quickly

**Ideal:**  
One calm sentence.

---

## Final product standard

Notifications should make users feel **more supported**, not **more managed**.
